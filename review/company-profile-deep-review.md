## Executive reading

**Subsequent source note:** the added notebook has now been reviewed separately in [notebook-design-review.md](notebook-design-review.md). The full profile presents an established Kenyan security business with a Mombasa head office, four named branch offices, a substantial guarding workforce, control-room-supported response and eight service lines. It provides considerably more substance than the initial five-service brief.

The strongest website story is how protection is delivered: personnel are vetted and trained, field activity is supervised, alarms reach a control room, mobile teams respond, incidents are documented and clients have account-management support. These are specific reasons to engage Winstar. A generic promise of safety would underuse the material.

The commercial proposal should therefore position the website as a tool for demonstrating operational credibility and generating qualified enquiries. The website should help a procurement or facilities team understand what Winstar can do, where it can do it, how it manages delivery and how to discuss a requirement.

## How the operation is described

### Guarding quality and reporting

PDF p.6 provides a useful sequence: vetting and interview by senior operations management; a three-week training programme; deployment; patrol checks and radio coordination; emergency dispatch; occurrence books, incident reports and daily summaries. Training topics include drill, access control, public relations, customer care and health, safety and environmental awareness.

This supports copy about preparation, supervision and reporting. It does not establish a specific background-check method, refresher schedule, independent training accreditation, client reporting portal or response-time SLA.

### Monitoring and response

PDF pp.6–7 and 9 describe control-room coordination across guarding, alarm response and cargo escort. PDF p.13 describes electric fences feeding alarm transmitters and mobile response. PDF p.11 describes CCTV/alarm integration. Together, these support a connected-protection story grounded in several services.

Do not translate these statements into an AI surveillance platform, universally monitored CCTV, an app, guaranteed prevention, armed response or fixed arrival time: none is established by the document. Confirm how the different services are actually combined commercially.

### Management structure

The image-based chart on PDF p.14 shows:

- Managing Director above General Manager, Administration & Technical, and General Manager, Operations & Logistics.
- Administration/Technical includes Finance & Accounts and Sales & Marketing. The latter leads to Sales Manager, Customer Care Officer and Technical Officer, with Alarms Technician under Technical Officer.
- Operations runs through Chief Operations Manager, Branch Coordinators and Duty Officer, then Supervisors, Controllers and Drivers. Guards and Dog Handlers sit below Supervisors; Alarm Back-up Crew below Controllers.
- Human Resource includes recruitment/training, office staffing/management and stores; outsourced cleaners appear beneath office staffing/management.

The chart has no named leaders. It supports a description of structured oversight, but not leadership biographies or claims that every service uses exclusively in-house personnel. The chart's outsourced cleaners are also relevant when defining facility-management delivery.

## Implications for the website proposal

1. **Expand the service scope from five to eight pages.** Use the actual profile taxonomy: Manned Guarding; Alarm Monitoring & Response; Canine Security; VIP & Cargo Escorts; Aviation Security; Electronic Security; Facility Management; Electric Fencing.
2. **Keep security as the main positioning.** Facility management should be visible as a complementary service with its own page and enquiry choice.
3. **Make accountable delivery a central content section.** Show vetting/training, supervision, monitoring/response, reporting and account management. The website should answer “How is this managed?” as well as “What do you sell?”
4. **Introduce evidence early.** Subject to currency and approval, use the history, workforce scale, office network, registration/membership and a small selection of client logos. The source now gives material to validate, rather than a blank evidence list.
5. **Preserve the original sector priorities.** Banking, real estate, aviation, warehousing and hospitality remain appropriate. Education and retail appear as possible secondary audiences from the client page, not automatic additions to launch scope.
6. **Use an enquiry journey suitable for varied contracts.** Ask about service, site location and need; route sales, recruitment and existing-client support separately. Confirm whether an assessment or a quote is the preferred first step.
7. **Treat regional footprint as a practical buying question.** Show approved offices and invite service-availability enquiries; do not turn all map labels into response hubs.
8. **Re-estimate implementation effort.** The previous 12-page draft becomes 15 pages with eight service pages. The earlier five-to-six-week estimate remains provisional until the expanded content and approvals are scoped.

The earlier competitor review already showed integrated protection as a common category message. The full profile does not justify claiming a unique service nobody else offers. It does give Winstar enough operational detail and reference material to present its own offer much more convincingly.

## Focused client follow-up

The most useful next input is approval of a fact sheet, rather than another broad discovery questionnaire: legal/trading name; founded/incorporated dates; dated staff counts; HQ plus branch details; current PSRA/KSIA evidence; service-specific coverage; preferred enquiry contacts; and three publishable client stories. Also confirm which eight services are commercial priorities now, and obtain original logo/photo assets with publication rights.

No independent verification of current registration, membership, client relationships or service coverage was performed in this document review. This does not negate the profile; it distinguishes supplied company evidence from independently checked facts.
