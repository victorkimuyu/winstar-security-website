# K9 security research and page decisions

Reviewed 6 November 2026. Implemented in `canine-security.html` using the existing service-page layout, with a profile photograph, two service descriptions, a handler and deployment section, four FAQs and an enquiry CTA.

| Primary source | What the provider or guidance emphasises | Applied to Winstar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [BM Security, Kenya](https://bmsecurity.com/solutions/k9-services/) | Patrol work and narcotics detection alongside dog and handler training, breeding and boarding. | Explain patrol and detection separately. Winstar’s profile does not establish standalone training, breeding or boarding services. |
| [SGA Security, East Africa](https://www.sgasecurity.com/our-services/security-dogs) | Patrol dogs paired with handlers, detection for defined substances, and integration with existing security procedures. | Present dog and handler as a team that fits the site’s security operation. Do not reuse comparisons with a fixed number of guards. |
| [ARN Security, Kenya](https://arnsecurity.co.ke/services/canine-services) | Planned patrol boundaries, site suitability, supervision and welfare. | Explain how routes, working hours and movement of people inform the assignment. Use settings as examples, not claims about specific clients. |
| [OCS, UK](https://ocs.com/uk/services/security-services/k9-security-dogs/) | Separates general patrol and specialist scent detection and explains site planning, safety, welfare and reporting. | Give both roles clear descriptions. Explain that the dog signals to the handler and the site’s agreed procedure determines what happens next. Do not imply UK qualifications or standards. |
| [Visual Security, Australia](https://www.visualsecuritynt.com.au/k9-security-units) | Patrols and deterrence, with explicit handler control and secure transport. | Address how deployment works around other site users without importing provider-specific policies or distances. |
| [Allied Universal, US](https://www.aus.com/security-services/enhanced-protection-services/k9-security) | Specialist detection teams organised around specific risks and training. | Describe only the target scents named in Winstar’s profile. Do not import US credentials, weapons screening or specialist deployment claims. |
| [UK NPSA, canine detection guidance](https://www.npsa.gov.uk/building-protection/search-screening/canine-detection-0) | Detection is task-specific: a trained dog and handler team indicates a defined scent in a suitable operating environment. | Explain indications in plain language and avoid guaranteed detection or promises that the dog identifies every threat. |

Winstar evidence: company profile PDF page 8 documents patrol and sniffer dogs, trained handlers, purpose-built deployment vehicles, medical care and nutrition. It names explosives and narcotics as detection targets and airports, stadiums and shopping centres as example settings. Notebook PDF page 4 corroborates dog–handler pairing and patrol/deterrence training. Winstar’s profile photo on PDF page 8 shows a handler and dog checking a vehicle; it is used as supplied.

The page uses common service language and avoids named certifications, quantified performance, breeding or boarding, and guaranteed coverage or availability. Venue and aviation examples describe potential applications, not current client deployments or approvals.
