# Winstar notebook: design and content review

Reviewed 24 September 2026. Source: `sources/Notebook 260126.pdf`, nine A5-sized PDF pages, all rendered and visually inspected. Page references use PDF order. The filename suggests 26 January 2026, but it does not establish client approval or authority over the company profile.

## Overall assessment

The notebook is a useful, more concise expression of Winstar's brand. Its strongest elements are the navy/red/light-blue palette, recognisable logo, operational photograph, service icons and short statements of capability. These offer a coherent foundation for the website and proposal presentation.

The website should adapt this visual language with clearer typography, more room for content and restrained decoration. The brightly coloured abstract cover is expressive stationery artwork; using it at the same scale throughout the website would compete with the company story and operational evidence.

## Website design direction

- Use white as the principal reading surface and navy for selected feature sections and the footer.
- Use red for primary actions and a limited number of section accents. Keep light blue for supporting graphics and consistent outline icons. Derive final colour specifications from approved brand assets, not screenshots.
- Feature Winstar personnel and operations with approved original photographs. The page-2 guard image supports the idea of a visible, reassuring presence; it does not by itself establish the pictured site's relationship to Winstar.
- Use one clear text family with a controlled heading hierarchy. The notebook mixes logo-like display lettering, condensed numerals, spaced capitals, body text and italic serif straplines; a website will benefit from fewer competing styles.
- Adapt the diagonal motif sparingly, especially in the footer or proposal cover. Keep text and calls to action on uncluttered surfaces.
- Give the six familiar services clear labels and icons. Extend the icon family consistently if the wider eight-service catalogue remains launch scope.
- Test actual colour contrast, text scaling, keyboard navigation and responsive layouts during implementation. This PDF review is not a website accessibility certification or a print-production preflight.

## Editorial and artwork corrections

- Change verb uses of “advice” to “advise,” or rewrite them plainly.
- Replace “security of a premise” with “security of premises.”
- Use consistent headings such as “Alarm Monitoring & Response” and “Canine Security.”
- Clarify the dog-training wording; “thorough-bred” and “suspect restraining” should not be copied directly into public service descriptions without operational review.
- Standardise “corporate & private” versus “corporate and private” in the recurring strapline.
- Keep the red facts readable against navy; avoid ultra-condensed numerals at small sizes.
- Confirm the isolated “i” marks on writing pages and the blank final page before a print reissue. No bleed, font-embedding or printer colour-profile checks were performed.

## Effect on the proposal

Retain “Proactive protection. Reassuring presence.” as the proposed website message, while recognising the existing “For all your corporate and private security needs” strapline. The new message remains a recommendation, not an approved replacement brand slogan.

Use the notebook as a visual reference and the profile as the fuller service source. Keep all eight service lines in the draft scope pending commercial prioritisation, while making CCTV and access control visible within electronic security and homepage navigation. The six highlighted notebook categories provide a useful alternative emphasis, not authority to drop services.

Before final public copy, resolve the Nairobi address, office count, values and approved client selection, and substantiate the instructor-certification claim. These are concrete reconciliation items introduced by the notebook.
